Moving to closed blocks (0.3)

TreeAMR 0.3 changes how a vertex-like dimension is owned (M22, "Closed blocks" in CODE.md). Under half-open ownership a block owned the points G+1 … G+N of a vertex-like dimension and its high face plane was a ghost; now it owns its closed range, G+1 … G+N+1, both face planes, and a point on a block face is a shared point with a copy on every block holding it, which the fill makes one number. Cell-centered and element sets (c = 0) are unchanged, except where this page says. This page lists every change an application or a companion package has to make, in one place.

The state and its size

  • A block's state is its closed box: ∏_d (N + c_d) points a variable, with c_d = 1 along a vertex-like dimension (staggers(fs)), where it was N^D. Size arrays and loops by N .+ staggers(fs), never by N. statelength, statevector, statearray (a plain reshape to (N+c_1, …, N+c_D, nvars, nblocks)), scatter! and gather! follow.
  • interiorview is the closed box. map_blocks! launches over it, every copy of every shared point; its closed keyword is gone, and passing it is refused with the reason.
  • The invariant is N ≥ 2G in every dimension (it was N ≥ 2G + 2 along a stagger).
  • Form every position through nodeposition (with block_lows and block_bases, or block_origins and block_spacings), so that the copies of a point are at one position bit for bit; a right-hand side that forms origin + (I − 1) h itself gives the copies positions that differ in the last bit.

Each point once: primaries

  • block_mapreduce, block_fold, mesh_mapreduce and mesh_fold over a set with a vertex-like dimension require primaries = true (each point once, at its primary copy) or false (every copy); there is no default. max, min and their like are right either way; a sum or a count needs true.
  • volume_weighted_norm and total_mass need no keyword: they take the trapezoid's weights within each block, half on a face plane, so a shared point has its weight once in total.
  • primary_mask(fs) and isprimary(mask, i…, b) say which copies are primaries. map_blocks! does not skip secondaries; a kernel that skips them through the mask must call unify!(du, …) afterwards, since the entries it leaves are not defined.

Walls

  • The walls keyword of FieldSet and GhostSchedule is gone, and so are symmetric walls and the derived wall row: the wall plane is an own point on both sides of a domain, evolved by default, the boundary hook filling the G ghosts beyond. A Dirichlet condition writes the wall too: CellBoundary(g; wall = :prescribed) (and boundary_by_coordinates(f; wall = :prescribed)), which writes the wall's primaries before the copies are unified. A downstream's own wall work-around (TreeGRRMHD's walls_to_state!, a zeroed wall du) goes.
  • At a reflecting face the wall plane is projected onto each variable's parity: an odd variable is zero there after every fill.

Unifying: unify! and UnifySchedule

  • restrict_interfaces!(flux, isched) and InterfaceSchedule are gone: the interface restriction of a conservative scheme is unify!(flux, UnifySchedule(flux)), which gives every secondary copy of a face value its primary's (the fine faces' area-weighted sum at a coarse-fine face). It takes no Operators.
  • unify!(v, fs, schedule) applies the same to a state vector; it is optional inside a run (the fill unifies the working array on every call, so no right-hand side reads a stale copy) but keeps a state's secondaries equal to their primaries, and is required after a right-hand side that skips secondaries.
  • Settle a state before reading it. The state vector an integrator returns holds each secondary as integrated, with its own block's stencil; only the next fill overwrites it. At a coarse-fine face the fine primary's stencil reads a prolongated ghost and the coarse secondary's does not, so their du differ by O(h^min(q, p−2)) (scheme order q, prolongation order p), which the secondary integrates with no feedback: on TreeWave's sine wave after a quarter period, π/2 at every N at p = 2, O(h²) at p = 4 and 6. An error norm, volume_weighted_norm or total_mass of a state, an output, a checkpoint, a symmetry check or a scatter! into regrid! without a fill reads those stale copies. Unify first, unify!(copy(u), fs, schedule) (across an interpolated glue it makes the copies their mean); where the hook prescribes a wall, unify! cannot write it, so scatter!, fill_ghosts! with the hook and gather! instead. An adaptive integrator's error estimate is not affected (measured: the same steps raw or unified). TreeAMR does not unify a state for you, at a step's end or in a right-hand side: that would cost every step of every run, and one unify! per read is the cheapest (Erik's decision, 2026-11-27).
  • Decide what a Dirichlet hook does with the wall. Under half-open ownership the low outer plane was evolved and the high one was a ghost the hook wrote; now both are own points, evolved unless the hook says wall = :prescribed. A port that keeps its hook unchanged evolves both. Where the data are the boundary condition itself, prescribe: TreeWave's closed ball, whose energy rose once by 7 % with an evolved sphere, follows its M20 history prescribed. Where the hook only supplies exact data around a scheme that converges anyway, evolving may keep more order: TreeBSSN's Kerr–Schild box and TreeWaveGR's holes converge faster evolved than prescribed, with somewhat larger errors. Say which, and why.
  • Write a state over the closed box. A helper that writes only the old owned range 1:N leaves the high-face copies as they were, and where those are the primaries the fill spreads the old values (TreeGRRMHD's gauge offset). Random data drawn per entry gives the copies of a point different values; draw from the point's position, or unify after.
  • Positions are nodeposition's, in tests too. A test comparing positions bit for bit with block_origins + (I − 1) h fails against the kernels' nodeposition. Every position is now formed from the patch's anchor and the block's integer corner, so two forests anchored at different points place the same physical point alike only to rounding (TreeWaveOLDG's nested boxes against a uniform box over the finest cube, element nodes off the integers: 3e-15 of the field). Where zero is on a patch's grid, positions are anchored at zero instead (step 10b): two domains that both have zero on their grid — a quadrant, bitant or half-turn domain and its full box — place a point alike bit for bit at any spacing, and a box centred on zero is exactly antisymmetric. Its edges are then ∓(n/2)·h, one ulp off the extents in about 6 % of boxes, always each other's negation. Off zero positions are counted from the patch's low corner, whose edge is the extent exactly, and the high edge lo + n·h is within two ulps of its extent; the face kinds play no part, so the same extents under other faces place every point alike. (An anchor at a symmetry face or the centre was tried and taken out again before 0.3.0: it put an ulp of the anchor on every point.)
  • A hand-written loop over interiorview that counts or sums visits every copy; skip the secondaries with isprimary(primary_mask(fs), i…, b), as primaries = true does.
  • After regrid!: rebuild the schedule, then fill or unify!, then gather!. regrid! leaves the unification to the next fill.

Seams, glues and pairs

  • On a rotating seam (a quarter turn) a set whose layout is not symmetric in the seam's plane and is vertex-like along either of its dimensions — a flux normal to one of them, with or without ghosts — has its seam copies in its partner set, so it is filled, unified and regridded only as a RotationPair; unify! of such a set alone is refused ("Unify it as a RotationPair"). Before 0.3 a flux without ghosts needed no partner.
  • A seam point's copies are one number through the turn, and a point on the axis is projected onto the turn's invariant part (a turned vector's in-plane components are zero there).
  • A reduced domain (quadrant, bitant, half turn) and its full box have the same positions bit for bit where zero is on both grids, and the same values to rounding: the reduced domain makes its seam's copies one number, where the box keeps the two values its data or right-hand side give at p and at its image. A test comparing the two compares to a bound that names that source; TreeGeneralizedHarmonic's spinning hole on the rotating quadrant measured 136–610 ulps on the seam planes after the first fill, amplified to 4.65e−9 by a moving layer.
  • Across a glue that copies, the lowest-numbered patch's copy is the primary; a glue may now reverse a vertex-like axis and join a high face to a high face. A set vertex-like along both dimensions of a patch corner (three or more patches meeting) is refused, whatever its ghost widths; interpolate it across its glues instead.
  • Across a glue a set is interpolated at (glue = :interpolate), every patch holding a point evolves its own copy, and the fill makes the copies their mean (stage A). M20's appendix is gone: statearray(u, fs; appendix = true) and map_blocks!(…; appendix) are refused with the reason, and isowned, hasappendix, corelength, appendix_entries, AppendixArray and the fold masks no longer exist; read the closed state as any other, and count with primaries = true.

Regridding

  • firing_boxes sweeps the closed box, so a point on a face fires in every block holding it, and along a vertex-like dimension it reports the cells touching the firing nodes: flag boxes stay in cell indices 1:N. A box an application builds by hand from a vertex-like dimension's nodes must likewise be the cells touching them.

Smaller changes

  • TreeAMR precompiles a fill (PrecompileTools, a new hard dependency): the package's own precompilation takes longer (2.3 → 16–21 s on a laptop, once per environment), and the first fill in a fresh process comes sooner (9.3 → 2.5 s after the start). An environment that takes TreeAMR from a checkout through [sources] needs Pkg.resolve() once to pick up the new dependency; Pkg.instantiate() alone leaves using TreeAMR failing to load PrecompileTools.

  • The fill runs its stages as A (kinked glues), W (prescribed walls), P (wall planes and a seam's axis), 0 (secondaries), 1, the hook, the interpolated glue ghosts, then 2; the boundary hook sees outer faces only.

  • A zero that a seam's turn or a wall's parity negates is +0, not −0.

  • interpolate! and locate_point read a point lying exactly on a face, edge or corner that leaves of two levels share from the finest leaf holding it, on either side of the face; before 0.3 the half-open rule gave it the upper leaf, coarse or fine. Among leaves of one level the upper one still holds it. A value interpolated at such a point moves by the scheme's error there, and its mirror image now comes from the same level.

  • A cell-centered set's numbers are unchanged; a vertex-like set's change where half-open ownership made a mesh asymmetric (refined regions, walls, seams), by the scheme's error there.

  • Checkpoints: TreeIOHDF5 stores a vertex-like set's closed blocks under the format feature closed_blocks, and refuses, saying why, a file written before 0.3 that holds such a set (M20's appendix and AMR-8's symmetric walls included); its cell-like sets still load through load_checkpoint(…; fieldsets). Cell and element files load as before. Erik decided (2026-11-26): every file written from 0.3 with a vertex-like set carries the feature closed_blocks; a half-open file with such a set, M20's interpolated_glue files included, is refused and not converted, since the planes it lacks are what a fill with the application's hook computes; and TreeIOHDF5 0.2.0, which bounds TreeAMR at =0.3.0, is released right after TreeAMR 0.3.0.
